This tool forecasts the society's budget for a whole academic year and lets you explore "what if?" questions: change a price, a pay rate, how many volunteers we have, whether we run a weekender, and watch the projected finances respond. Every default below is drawn from our real records; the reasoning behind each number is in the references at the bottom.

How to read the ranges

The shaded band around the line above is a confidence interval. Attendance and some costs can't be known exactly in advance, so the balance is drawn as a best estimate plus a spread rather than a single line: with the confidence set to 95% (the default, adjustable in the Analysis controls), the true balance should stay inside the band about 95 times out of 100 if our assumptions hold. A wider band means more uncertainty. The same shaded ranges appear on the charts further down (Level 1 sign-ups) and on the year result at the top. The probability of a surplus is the chance the year finishes in the black rather than at a loss. (what is a confidence interval?)

Controls

The controls are grouped into themed sections: classes, socials, the weekender, volunteers and so on. Click a section heading to expand or collapse it, then change any value to update the forecast. Each control has an ? explaining where its default came from. Values update after a short pause, so the server isn't flooded while you adjust several at once. Each section has its own Reset section to best guess button; Reset to best guess below returns every control to its data-driven default.

Under the bonnet: assumptions, dates and attendance models

The forecast above rests on a generated calendar and a few statistical models fitted to our attendance history. Every control's default is our best guess: the value drawn from our own records (attendance, past prices, real band and bank figures) that we think most likely, so "reset to best guess" returns the whole tool to that data-driven starting point. The assumptions and models here are shown for scrutiny; you don't need them to use the tool.

Key assumptions

The forecast is only as good as the assumptions behind it. The main ones:

  • Price does not change attendance. The model holds turnout fixed when you change a price, so raising a price raises income proportionally. In reality a big rise would put some people off; treat large price changes as an upper bound on the gain.
  • Attendance follows the recent past. Level 1 sign-ups, Level 2, social and weekender turnout are projected from the last few years' records. A step change in the scene (a surge of new dancers, a rival class opening) is not anticipated.
  • The calendar follows our usual rules (six terms of 5–7 weeks around the two fixed parties, socials spread evenly). The generated dates are shown below.
  • Costs are the current real figures and stay flat across the year except where a figure is explicitly inflation-adjusted. No mid-year price rises from venues or suppliers are assumed.
  • Level 1 and Level 2 are paid for differently. Level 1 beginners buy a whole term up front (unless you switch on night-by-night selling, which then applies the term's attendance drop-off). Level 2 is not a term block: it is sold per night, so its income tracks how many dancers turn up each Thursday, modelled from the per-night attendance history. Level 2 loyalty refunds follow a fitted attendance survival curve, not a flat share of revenue, because most dancers come only a few nights.
  • The weekender audience is last time's, minus the one-off crowd. The 30th anniversary weekender drew 195 attendees; the recurring audience is taken as the ~45% who kept coming to events afterwards (~87 people), not the whole one-off crowd. That audience is split into full-pass / day-pass / social-pass / single-social buyers by what each dancer actually did at the 30th (classes vs evenings attended), then scaled to the audience size you set. The evenings-only social pass is a real ticket the 30th made plain, where the socials drew far more people than the daytime classes.

Generated term and event dates

The calendar is worked out from fixed rules rather than typed in, so it moves correctly with the forecast year. The autumn term starts on the second Thursday of September; the Christmas party is the last class Thursday before Christmas and the end-of-year party the last Thursday of June; the Thursdays in between are cut into six terms of five to seven weeks, as even as possible, skipping Easter week. Tea dances (and the weekender, if run) are spaced evenly between the two parties: tea dances snap to the nearest Sunday, the weekender to a Friday. The timeline shows the shaded terms with each class night, and the socials as labelled markers.

References & where the numbers come from

The nominal (best-guess) figures are drawn from these sources:

  • Attendance figures (Level 1 sign-ups, Level 2 and social turnout, the weekender's repeat audience, loyalty refunds) are fitted from the society's own attendance database: the same records behind the Attendance page.
  • Band fee is a distribution fitted to eight real band payments from the 2023–25 bank statements (mean about £717), each uprated for inflation.
  • Membership is the mean of the paid-member counts for 2024–26 (64, 85, 66); their year-to-year spread sets its uncertainty.
  • Weekender ticket mix (full pass, day pass, social pass, single social) is inferred from the 30th-anniversary weekender, where the evening socials drew far more people than the daytime classes.
  • Prices, pay rates, room hours, the opening bank balance and overheads are the committee's current real figures, held in a private spreadsheet.
  • Inflation uses the ONS Consumer Prices Index, 12-month rate (series D7G7).
  • Ticketing fees: Dancecloud 2.5% + 50p per ticket (capped £5), and Stripe 1.5% + 20p charged separately on the daily transfer to our bank.
  • The confidence ranges are produced cheaply with the unscented transform (the sigma-point method at the heart of the unscented Kalman filter), which is fast enough to update as you edit.
