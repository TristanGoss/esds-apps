This tool forecasts the society's budget for a whole academic year and lets you explore "what if?" questions — change a price, a pay rate, how many volunteers we have, whether we run a weekender, and watch the projected finances respond. It is a production port of the forecasting notebook the committee has been building; every default below is drawn from our real records, and the reasoning behind each number is in the references at the bottom.

Reading the uncertainty

Attendance and some costs can't be known exactly in advance, so most figures here are given as a best estimate plus a range rather than a single number. That range is a confidence interval: with the confidence set to 95% (the default), it's the band the true figure will fall inside about 95 times out of 100 if our assumptions hold. A wider band means more uncertainty. You can change the confidence level in the Analysis controls. (what is a confidence interval?)

The ranges are worked out with a technique called the unscented transform, which is fast enough to update as you type — see the references. The probability of a surplus is the chance the year finishes in the black rather than at a loss.

Money in the bank through the year

The projected current-account balance from today's opening balance to next summer. The shaded band is the confidence interval — the balance is likely to stay inside it. The dotted line marks the opening balance, so you can see at a glance whether the year adds to or draws down the reserves.

Controls

Change any value to update the forecast. Each control has an ? explaining where its default came from. Values update after a short pause so the server isn't flooded while you adjust several at once. Use Reset to forecast to return every control to its data-driven default.

Under the bonnet: the attendance models

The forecast above rests on a few statistical models fitted to our attendance history. They are shown here for scrutiny; you don't need them to use the tool.

Level 1 sign-ups per term

How many people we expect to sign up for beginners' classes each term, with a confidence range. Sign-ups have been declining year on year and are largest in the autumn and January terms; the model captures both. Grey dots are the actual past terms it was fitted to.

Level 2 loyalty: how many free tickets?

Every 8th Level 2 ticket a dancer buys is refunded. Most dancers come only a few nights, so the refunds come from the regulars. This survival curve shows the share of dancers attending at least a given number of nights; the refund count is read off where the loyalty threshold falls. The dashed line is the fitted model, the dots the real data.

Where the uncertainty comes from

Not every unknown matters equally. This ranks how much each uncertain input contributes to the spread in the year result, so it's clear which number would be most worth pinning down.

References & where the numbers come from

The nominal (default) figures are drawn from these sources:

  • Attendance figures (Level 1 sign-ups, Level 2 and social turnout, the weekender's repeat audience, loyalty refunds) are fitted from the society's own attendance database — the same records behind the Attendance page.
  • Band fee is a distribution fitted to eight real band payments from the 2023–25 bank statements (mean about £672), each uprated for inflation.
  • Membership is the mean of the paid-member counts for 2024–26; its year-to-year spread sets its uncertainty.
  • Prices, pay rates, room hours, the opening bank balance and overheads are the committee's current real figures, held in a private spreadsheet off GitHub.
  • Inflation uses the ONS Consumer Prices Index, 12-month rate (series D7G7).
  • Ticketing fees: Dancecloud 2.5% + 50p per ticket (capped £5), and Stripe 1.5% + 20p charged separately on the daily transfer to our bank.
  • The unscented transform (used to produce the confidence ranges cheaply) is the sigma-point method at the heart of the unscented Kalman filter.

The forecasting notebook remains the design surface and the fuller write-up; this page is a faithful port of it for interactive use.
